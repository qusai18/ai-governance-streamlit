Agent Action Gate

Guardrails are the checks that keep an AI agent safe to run: they inspect what goes in, decide whether the agent’s action may happen, inspect what comes out, and learn from every decision. Pick a call, run it through, and tap any check to see what it guards against and which rules it supports.

How guardrails think

Guardrails don’t trust the model to police itself. A model can be tricked by what it reads, be confidently wrong, or get stuck in a loop, so the safety decisions are made by checks outside it. They run in a fixed order: input guardrails, then the action gate, then output guardrails, with a person as the safety valve and monitoring around all of it.

  • Deny by default: only listed tools, and only records the user is entitled to.
  • Within each stage, cheap and absolute checks run first; judgment calls run last.
  • Risk depends on the action, not the agent. Reading an order isn’t wiring money.
  • Anything irreversible or high-impact goes to a person, and silence counts as no.
  • Every guardrail trades safety for friction. Tune with real feedback, not guesses.
  • Every decision is logged with the check that made it, so you can audit and improve.
Which guardrail types this covers
Security
Prompt injection, the allow-list, record scope, payee and first-contact checks.
Safety
Harmful intent in requests and harmful content in replies.
Policy
Off-limits topics and payment approval limits.
Privacy
Redaction on the way in and out, and who may see sensitive data.
Output quality
The confidence bar for writes and a grounding check on replies.
Bias and fairness
Partial: a wording check on replies only. Real coverage needs evaluation sets and outcome monitoring across groups.
Compliance
Each check names the regulation or framework it supports, and the audit log is your evidence. This is a teaching model, not a certification.
